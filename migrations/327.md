# Migrating from 1.0.0-alpha.327

## Calls to a component arrive on one queue

Every ordinary call to a component arrives on `<namespace>.<component>..requests`, where each
operation had a queue of its own, `<namespace>.<component>.<operation>`. Nothing changes in what a
component declares or calls. See [calls](/documentation/calls.md).

What this means for what you run:

- **This release is not deployed by a rolling update.** A process of this release and a process of
  the one before do not reach each other: a call made by one is not taken by the other, and its
  caller waits. Deploy through a [halt](/documentation/halt.md), or with the old replicas stopped
  before the new ones start.
- **The queues of the release before are left on the broker**, one per operation, consumed by
  nothing. They are what this release exists to remove, and nothing removes them but you.
- **Calls of one component share what a process holds unanswered**: 300 for the component, where
  it was 300 for each of its operations. An operation slow enough to fill that belongs in a
  component of its own.
- **A call for an operation a component does not have is answered** with an `EndpointException`,
  where it waited.

Once every process of the deployment runs this release, list what is left — durable queues nothing
consumes, whose names have no `..` in them and are not comq's own:

```shell
API=http://<host>:15672/api VHOST=%2F AUTH=<user>:<password>

curl -su "$AUTH" "$API/queues/$VHOST?columns=name,durable,consumers" |
  jq -r '.[] | select(.durable and .consumers == 0
    and (.name | contains("..") | not)
    and (.name | startswith("comq.") | not)) | .name' > leftover.txt
```

Read the list: a queue of your own that nothing happens to be consuming, such as one a receiver of
a component that is scaled to zero reads from, is on it too. Then delete what is on it:

```shell
xargs -a leftover.txt -I{} curl -su "$AUTH" -X DELETE "$API/queues/$VHOST/{}"
```

1. Deploy through a halt, or stopping the old replicas first.
2. Delete the per-operation queues, in every virtual host a deployment uses.
