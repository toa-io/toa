# Migrating to 1.0.0-alpha.263

Every deployment now states what it may take. One that states nothing is refused at export.

## Why

A pod without `resources` is `BestEffort`: the first evicted under memory pressure and the
last given CPU under contention. On a busy node that is slow enough for the pod to fail its
own startup probe, restart, and fail it again — while the chart reports nothing wrong and
`helm --wait` sits there until it times out.

This is not hypothetical. It is how the extension added in the previous release reached
production: the annotation supports `resources`, nobody wrote any, and the explorer went out
as `BestEffort`. Nothing said so — not the export, not the chart, not the rollout.

An omission and a decision looked the same. Now they do not.

## What changed

`toa export deployment` and `toa deploy` fail when a composition, a service or a mono
deployment has no resources:

```
Composition 'one' declares no resources. Declare them on it or as the context's
'resources', or 'resources: null' to deploy it without any.
```

## What to do

State them where the deployment is declared:

```yaml
# context.toa.yaml

compositions:
  - name: one
    resources:
      cpu: [200m, 1]
      memory: [200Mi, 500Mi]
    components:
      - one.two

exposition:
  resources:
    cpu: [200m, 2]
    memory: [200Mi, 1Gi]
```

A composition Toa derives for you — one it created because nothing declared it — cannot
carry its own, so state the context's default. It applies to any deployment that states
nothing of its own:

```yaml
resources:
  cpu: [200m, 1]
  memory: [200Mi, 500Mi]
```

To deploy without any — for a local context, or a deployment that genuinely wants none —
say so:

```yaml
resources: null
```

`null` may be stated at either place, and a deployment's own answer wins over the context's.

## Choosing the numbers

The requests are the part that matters, and they are not a guess at idle usage. A pod is
guaranteed its request and nothing more, so the request has to cover the worst moment,
which is starting up: schemas compile, indexes are created, connections are established.
During a rollout every pod does this at once, and the spare CPU that usually absorbs it is
not there.

Read the startup time off a healthy pod — from `Starting composition` to `Ready` — and
divide by the probe's budget. A composition that takes 74 seconds against a 300 second
budget needs at least a quarter of a core guaranteed to survive contention, and a request
that low leaves the cluster autoscaler blind: it adds nodes when pods do not fit by
request, never when running pods are throttled.
