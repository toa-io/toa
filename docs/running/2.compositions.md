# Compositions

[The Big Picture](../foundations/2.overview.md) introduced the composition as the physical
boundary of an application: a group of components running as one process. This article looks at
the composition as the unit an operator works with.

## The unit of deployment and scale

A composition is what gets built, deployed, and replicated.

```yaml
# context.toa.yaml
compositions:
  - name: core
    components: [orders, billing, inventory]
    replicas: 3
  - name: reports
    components: [reports]
    replicas: 1
```

Every replica of a composition runs all of its components. Scaling `core` to three replicas
gives three processes, each serving `orders`, `billing`, and `inventory`. Components in one
composition scale together, restart together, and share the resources the composition is given.

A component that no composition lists is given a composition of its own. Leaving compositions
out entirely therefore yields one process per component, and listing every component in one
composition yields a single process. Both are valid starting points.

## What grouping decides

Grouping is an operational decision, and several independent considerations bear on it.

**Calls between members stay in memory.** Components that call each other often are cheaper
together.

**Members share fate.** A defect that exhausts memory in one component takes down the process
and everything in it. Isolation is bought with separate compositions.

**Members share a scale.** A component with a very different load from its neighbors forces
them to be replicated with it, or it with them.

**Members share a release.** A composition is rebuilt and redeployed when any of its members
changes.

**Members share a queue of attention.** Work that takes minutes, such as long
[tasks](../reliability/4.tasks.md), competes with the short work of its neighbors.

None of these appears in component code, so the decision can be changed as evidence
accumulates. The [topology](../platform/4.introspection.md) shows which components actually
talk to each other, which is the best input for it.

## Replicas

Replicas of a composition are interchangeable. They take calls from the same queues and events
from the same subscriptions, and each call or event is handled by one of them.

Two facts about replicas matter to how an application behaves.

**More than one is the normal case.** The runtime's guarantees are designed for it, and running
a single replica removes the redundancy they assume. Deployments default to more than one.

**Replicas of different versions coexist.** During a rollout, old and new replicas serve
together for a time. [Contracts](3.contracts.md) describes what that requires.

## Services in a composition

The services that extensions provide — the gateway and others — are processes too. By default
each is deployed on its own. A composition can instead include a service, which then runs
inside the composition's process:

```yaml
compositions:
  - name: edge
    components: [orders]
    services: [exposition]
```

This is the same trade as grouping components. Fewer processes cost less to run and are simpler
to operate; separate ones are isolated and scale independently. At one end of the range is the
single-process form, where every component and every service runs together.

## Leaving things out

An application is sometimes larger than what one deployment should run. A component may be
operated by another team on other infrastructure, or run on special hardware outside the
cluster.

Such a component can be *evicted* from a deployment. It remains part of the application: other
components call it by name, receive its events, and are held to its contract. The deployment
does not start it, and something else is expected to.

Eviction keeps the logical architecture whole where the physical architecture is not uniform.
The application is still described once, and the description says which parts this deployment
takes responsibility for.

## Resources

A composition states what it may consume in processor time and memory, or inherits a default
from the Context. These limits are part of the declaration for the same reason compositions
are: they are properties of how the application runs, they vary by
[environment](1.environments.md), and they belong with the other deployment decisions.
